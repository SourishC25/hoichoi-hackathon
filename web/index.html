How Birati decides where, whether and what

    
    
      
  1. Perception (deterministic, frame-precise). Camera cuts (ffmpeg scdet), speech activity
        (Silero VAD — language-agnostic, so Bengali/English code-switching doesn't matter), loudness, fades to black.
    2. 
      
  2. Global understanding (Gemini, whole episode, audio + video). Groups shots into semantically
        coherent scenes; for each: summary, dominant activity, mood, and sensitive topics — using a vocabulary
        built at runtime from every brand's negative_contexts.
    3. 
      
  3. WHERE. Each scene boundary / fade is snapped to a frame-exact camera cut inside a speech-free gap.
        No clean cut nearby → rejected. Mid-sentence cuts are structurally impossible. Gemini then re-watches
        a 42-second clip around every cut as a broadcast editor: is dialogue continuing? is the beat complete?
    4. 
      
  4. WHAT. Gemini scores every brand for the slot (dominant activity wins) and lists violations.
        Negative contexts are a hard, fail-closed block from three independent layers: global scene tags,
        lead-in clip review, and an adversarial verifier on the final pick.
    5. 
      
  5. WHETHER. An exact dynamic programme picks the best set of breaks under max breaks/hour,
        min gap, and the ad-load budget. Weak moments get no break at all.
    6. 
      
  6. Output. IAB VMAP 1.0 with inline VAST 3.0, a debug JSON explaining every decision, and this
        player, which reads the VMAP, cuts on the exact frame, plays the ad and resumes.
    7.
